Topic 5.4: Selection of Categories of Psychological Disorders

Last Updated: July 13, 2026
← Topic 5.3 Table of Contents Topic 5.5 →

The Big Picture: Categorizing the Mind

Welcome to the longest and most detailed topic in AP Psychology! Here we transition from the theory of diagnosing into the specific categories outlined in manuals like the DSM. While there are countless disorders diagnosed by professionals, the AP exam focuses on a core representative list to give you an introductory understanding of psychopathology. Let's break down the symptoms and causes for these major categories.

1. Neurodevelopmental Disorders

Neurodevelopmental Disorders are a group of disorders with onset occurring during the developmental period (childhood). Symptoms focus on whether the person is exhibiting behaviors appropriate for their age or maturity range. Possible causes may be environmental, physiological, or genetic.

  • Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD): Characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that interfere with functioning or development.
  • Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD): Characterized by persistent difficulties in social communication and interaction, along with restricted or repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities.

2. Schizophrenic Spectrum Disorders

Schizophrenic Spectrum Disorders are characterized by issues in one or more of five areas: delusions, hallucinations, disorganized thinking/speech, disorganized motor behavior, and negative symptoms. The disorder can be experienced as an acute (sudden) or chronic (long-term) condition. Possible causes suggest a genetic/biological link, such as prenatal virus exposure or imbalances with certain neurotransmitters (this is called the Dopamine Hypothesis, which points to excessive dopamine activity in brain pathways).

Positive Symptoms (Adding Abnormal Behaviors)

Positive Symptoms involve the presence of abnormal experiences.

  • Delusions: Strongly held false beliefs. For example, Delusions of Persecution (believing someone is out to get you) or Delusions of Grandeur (believing you are exceptionally powerful or famous).
  • Hallucinations: False sensory experiences, such as hearing voices or seeing things that aren't there.
  • Disorganized Speech: Incoherent or fragmented speech. In severe cases, it manifests as a Word Salad (stringing together words in nonsensical ways).

Negative Symptoms and Motor Behavior

Negative Symptoms present as the lack of a typical behavior.

  • Flat Affect: Lack of outward emotional expression.
  • Catatonia: Disordered movement that may be experienced as excitement (a positive symptom) or as a Catatonic Stupor (a negative symptom where an individual is immobile and unresponsive).

3. Depressive Disorders

Depressive Disorders involve the presence of sad, empty, or irritable mood along with physical and cognitive changes that impair a person's ability to function. Causes range from biological/genetic sources to cognitive schemas.

  • Major Depressive Disorder: Characterized by persistent depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure that significantly impairs functioning for at least two weeks.
  • Persistent Depressive Disorder: A chronic form of depression lasting for at least two years.

4. Bipolar Disorders

Bipolar disorders are defined by Cycling, which involves experiencing periods of depression and Mania (a state of abnormally elevated mood, energy, and impulsive activity) in alternating periods.

  • Bipolar I Disorder: Characterized by at least one full manic episode, which may be preceded/followed by depressive episodes.
  • Bipolar II Disorder: Characterized by at least one major depressive episode and at least one episode of Hypomania (a period of elevated mood/energy that is less severe than full mania), without ever having a full manic episode.

5. Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety Disorders feature excessive fear and/or anxiety with related disturbances to behavior. Causes often focus on learned associations (conditioning), maladaptive thinking, and genetic sources.

  • Specific Phobia: An intense, irrational fear of a specific object or situation. Examples include Acrophobia (fear of heights) or Arachnophobia (fear of spiders).
  • Agoraphobia: Intense fear of specific social situations where escape might be difficult (using public transit, being in crowds, being in open/enclosed spaces).
  • Panic Disorder: Involves the experience of a Panic Attack (unanticipated, overwhelming episodes of intense fear, rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath). It can manifest as culture-bound syndromes like Ataque de Nervios in Latin American cultures.
  • Social Anxiety Disorder: Intense fear of being judged or watched by others in social settings. Distinct from agoraphobia. A culture-bound manifestation in Japan is Taijin Kyofusho, an intense fear of offending or embarrassing others through one's appearance or body odor.
  • Generalized Anxiety Disorder: Involves prolonged, continuous experiences of nonspecific anxiety or fear (being continually tense and apprehensive).

6. Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

These disorders involve unwanted mental intrusions and repetitive actions.

  • Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD): Characterized by the presence of Obsessions (persistent, intrusive, and unwanted thoughts) and Compulsions (intrusive, repetitive behaviors intended to address the obsessions/reduce anxiety).
  • Hoarding Disorder: Persistent difficulty discarding possessions, regardless of value, resulting in excessive clutter.

7. Dissociative Disorders

Dissociative Disorders are characterized by disruptions or dissociations from consciousness, memory, identity, emotion, or perception. Causes primarily involve severe trauma or stress.

  • Dissociative Amnesia: An inability to recall important personal information, usually related to trauma. Sometimes accompanied by a Fugue (unexpected travel away from home with amnesia for one's identity).
  • Dissociative Identity Disorder: Characterized by the presence of two or more distinct identity states that control behavior, along with gaps in memory.

8. Trauma and Stressor-Related Disorders

These are caused by exposure to a traumatic or stressful event.

  • Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD): Characterized by subsequent psychological distress involving Hypervigilance (heightened alertness/scanning for threats), severe anxiety, Flashbacks (vivid experiences of reliving the trauma), insomnia, emotional detachment, and hostility.

9. Feeding and Eating Disorders

These disorders deal with altered consumption or absorption of food that impairs physical health or psychological functioning.

  • Anorexia Nervosa: Severe restriction of food intake, intense fear of gaining weight, and distorted body image leading to significantly low body weight.
  • Bulimia Nervosa: Recurrent episodes of binge eating followed by compensatory behaviors like vomiting, fasting, or excessive exercise to prevent weight gain.

10. Personality Disorders

Personality Disorders are enduring, inflexible patterns of thinking, feeling, and behaving that deviate from cultural expectations, begin in adolescence/early adulthood, and lead to impairment. They are divided into three distinct clusters:

Cluster A: Odd or Eccentric

  • Paranoid Personality Disorder: Pervasive distrust and suspicion of others.
  • Schizoid Personality Disorder: Persistent detachment from social relationships and limited emotional expression.
  • Schizotypal Personality Disorder: Odd beliefs, eccentric behavior, and discomfort in close relationships.

Cluster B: Dramatic, Emotional, or Erratic

  • Antisocial Personality Disorder: Persistent disregard for and violation of the rights of others (deceitfulness, impulsivity, lack of remorse).
  • Borderline Personality Disorder: Instability in mood, self-image, and relationships, with intense fear of abandonment.
  • Histrionic Personality Disorder: Excessive emotionality and attention-seeking behavior.
  • Narcissistic Personality Disorder: Inflated sense of self-importance, need for excessive admiration, and lack of empathy.

Cluster C: Anxious or Fearful

  • Avoidant Personality Disorder: Extreme social inhibition and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation despite a desire for connection.
  • Dependent Personality Disorder: Excessive need to be taken care of, leading to submissive/clinging behavior.
  • Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder: Pervasive pattern of perfectionism, rigid orderliness, and need for control (distinct from OCD as it lacks true obsessions/compulsions).

Don't Trip Up! (Common Misconceptions)

⚠️ Bipolar I vs. Bipolar II: Don't get these confused! Bipolar I requires at least one full manic episode. Bipolar II requires a major depressive episode and hypomania (a milder form of mania), but never a full manic episode.

⚠️ Schizophrenia vs. DID: Schizophrenia involves a split from reality (psychosis, hallucinations, delusions). Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) involves a split in identities. They are completely different categories of disorders!
