💡 Getting the Most Out of Flashcards

Use Mark as Known aggressively — the goal is to shrink your deck down to the terms you're actually struggling with, not to flip through everything on autopilot. Try switching to Definition First mode occasionally too; being handed a definition and recalling the term is closer to how AP Psych questions actually work than the reverse. Every term here is also the same one bolded the first time it appears in our Topic Overview pages, so if a card isn't sticking, it's worth reading that term in context there rather than just re-drilling the definition in isolation. And as you get closer to the exam, study by mixing units together instead of one at a time — interleaved practice like that is consistently shown to build stronger long-term recall than reviewing a single unit in isolation.

📖 Terms in This Section

Confirmation Bias — A tendency to search for information that supports our preconceptions and to ignore or distort contradictory evidence.
Hindsight Bias — The tendency to believe, after an event has occurred, that you could have predicted the outcome in advance (“I knew it all along” phenomenon)
Overconfidence — The tendency to be more confident than correct—to overestimate the accuracy of our beliefs and judgments.
Psychology — The scientific study of behavior and mental processes.
Case Study — A descriptive technique in which one individual or group is studied in depth in the hope of revealing universal principles.
Convenience Sample — A sample of participants selected because they are easy to access or readily available rather than randomly chosen from the population, which can make it less representative of the general population
Experimenter Bias — The influence of the experimenter's expectations or preferences on the outcome of a research study.
Generalizability — The extent to which research findings can be applied to larger populations, other settings, or different groups beyond the participants in the study
